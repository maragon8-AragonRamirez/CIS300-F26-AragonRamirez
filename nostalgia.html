Memory, meaning, and the past

The science of nostalgia

Nostalgia is more than simply remembering. Researchers describe it as a sentimental reflection on meaningful parts of our past—often bittersweet, and often involving people who matter to us.

7 studies

Memory is often social

Across seven studies, nostalgic stories commonly featured the self with close others. Experimental studies also found increases in reported social bonds, positive self-regard, and positive affect.

Wildschut et al., 2006

521 people

A recent research sample

A 2025 study examined 521 participants (average age 27.7). A score for spatial details in nostalgic memories was positively associated with well-being after accounting for anxiety.

Likhanov et al., 2025

Not a cure

Context matters

Nostalgia can feel warm and comforting, but it can also feel bittersweet. Research findings do not show that nostalgia cures distress or that making a time capsule improves mental health.

A careful read of the evidence

What the studies suggest

In Wildschut and colleagues' 2006 experiments, negative mood and loneliness could prompt nostalgia. Their study series also found that nostalgic reflection could strengthen reported social connection and positive feelings about the self. The authors described nostalgia as a social emotion, because important memories often include close relationships.

The 2025 study by Likhanov and colleagues looked at nostalgia with spatial details—such as remembering places and scenes. The link to well-being was an association in questionnaire data, not proof that nostalgia caused better well-being. The researchers also reported that the relationship did not remain when neuroticism was controlled for.
